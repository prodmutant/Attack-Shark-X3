        DPI accuracy

        Lay a ruler on the desk. Press Start, slide the mouse exactly the distance along it in one straight stroke, then press Stop. Lift the mouse to go back. Do runs both left-right and up-down to compare the two axes.

        Lift-off

        Raise the mouse by stacking flat things under both sides, level, so the sensor sits that far off the pad: a playing card is about 0.3 mm, printer paper about 0.1 mm. Enter the height, press Test and slide it back and forth for four seconds. Lift-off is set to -.

        Top speed and spin-out

        Press Start and flick as hard as you would in a game, several times, then Stop. Speed uses the active DPI stage, so check DPI accuracy first if it matters.

          Path

          Press Start and draw: slow diagonals, circles, quick zig-zags. Each dot is one report. Then flip angle snap and draw the same shapes to see what it does. Angle snap is -.

          Stillness

          Put the mouse down on the pad, press Start and do not touch it or the desk. A healthy sensor sends nothing. Try it at your highest DPI stage, where noise shows first.

          Click timing

          Press Start, then click every button the way you normally do for twenty seconds: singles, doubles, fast taps. Macros are paused while this runs.

          Scroll wheel

          Scroll one way only, one notch at a time, and count the notches as you go. Enter the count before you stop and the page compares it with what arrived. Notches that register in the wrong direction are the usual first sign of a worn wheel.

          Sensitivity

          How far the mouse travels for one full turn in a game. The yaw is the game's degrees per count at sensitivity 1; 0.022 is the most common value. Games that read raw input ignore the Windows pointer speed.

          Windows

          Host settings that change what the mouse sends before anything sees it, and software that fights over it.

          Per-app profiles

          A profile overrides some settings while one of its programs is the window in front, and hands the rest back when you switch away. The dashboard keeps showing your normal settings.
